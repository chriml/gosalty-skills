# Kite-Surfer Behavior Reference

Use this reference to ground GoSalty discovery work in how kite surfers make
decisions before, during, and after a session.

## Decision Model

Kite surfers rarely decide from a single data point. They combine forecast,
local spot knowledge, personal skill, gear, tide or water state, social proof,
travel time, and safety constraints.

Common questions:

- Is there enough wind for my gear and skill level?
- Is the wind direction safe for this spot?
- Are tide, current, waves, obstacles, launch space, and rescue options okay?
- How far away is the spot, and is the session window worth the trip?
- What do locals or recent riders say?
- Is this spot beginner-friendly, advanced-only, crowded, restricted, or
  seasonal?
- Are lessons, rentals, storage, parking, food, showers, or rescue services
  available?

## Behavior Patterns

- Forecast scanning is time-sensitive. Riders often compare today, tomorrow,
  and the next few windy windows instead of browsing static destination pages.
- Skill and gear change the answer. A spot or wind range that is perfect for one
  rider can be unsafe or useless for another.
- Trust matters. Recent reports, local knowledge, photos, source labels, and
  clear timestamps reduce hesitation.
- Safety beats convenience. Offshore wind, gusty conditions, shallow obstacles,
  restricted zones, difficult launch areas, or no rescue access should change
  ranking, messaging, and calls to action.
- Travel riders need logistics. They care about seasonality, nearby stays,
  schools, rental availability, airport access, transport, and what to pack.
- Local riders need speed. They want quick answers, alerts, and confidence that
  the app understands their favorite spots and conditions.
- Community content must be specific. "Good spot" is less useful than launch
  direction, wind direction fit, hazard notes, local rules, and recent condition
  reports.

## Product Implications

- Make condition fit explicit instead of assuming all wind is good wind.
- Prefer personalized wording when skill level, gear, or favorites are known.
- Show timestamps and source quality for forecast, reports, and spot facts.
- Treat missing safety data as an unknown, not as safe.
- Separate inspiration browsing from ride-now decisions.
- Keep partner and event workflows grounded in whether they help a rider choose,
  plan, attend, or trust an on-water experience.
